## Prompt 17

> Of all the professions for a Filipino source, nurse was chosen. Why?
> And for the Vietnamese source, nail bar owner. Why?

### Response 17

Both choices repeat occupational stereotypes, which the game's brief asked me to avoid.

- **Filipino nurse:** this came from your example ("a Filipino nurse union rep"), and I used it without questioning it. Filipino nurses are one of the largest groups of internationally recruited NHS staff, so it's realistic. But Marites is the only Filipino source, so the game links Filipino people with nursing. Her second question, about moving from the Philippines to nurse, makes that link stronger.
- **Vietnamese nail bar owner:** this one was entirely my choice, and it's the more obvious stereotype. I needed a second shop owner for the shoplifting story and went for the cliché.

Possible fixes:

1. **Linh Nguyen:** make her the owner of the village bakery or hardware shop. Nothing in the story needs a particular kind of shop.
2. **Marites Santos:** either
   - keep her as a nurse but drop the feature about moving from the Philippines, so her nationality isn't the point of her role, or
   - change her profession. She could be a rail union rep, a council housing officer, or an engineer asked about a bridge closure. The strike-vote question then needs a different union.
3. Have a Filipino or Vietnamese source appear as an expert or in a position of power somewhere else, so East and Southeast Asian people aren't only shown in service and care work.

Which would you like? I'd do 1, 2 with the change of profession, and 3.
