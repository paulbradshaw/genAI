## Prompt 20

> Here's some background on the BBC's 50:50 project - identify any relevant sections or pages where this might be incorporated.

*(Attachments: BBC 50:50 "How it works", "Our Partners" and "BBC Stories" pages)*

### Response 20

I've looked through the three BBC pages and found eight places in the game where the 50:50 material fits. One of them is an answer the BBC material contradicts, so it needs fixing. I read only the start of the "Our Partners" page, which is very long, and these pages date from around 2021–22.

**Needs fixing**
1. **Level 5, counting disabled sources (5.10).** I marked "asking every source" as intrusive. But BBC Radio Wales asked callers directly and found "very few people objected". It also matters that 50:50 mostly monitors by perception and only uses self-ID forms where teams can reach every contributor. The answer should be: record what sources tell you; where you can ask, ask briefly and explain why; don't guess from what you can see.

**New question**
2. **"Measure what you control"** would fit level 5 well and ties straight back to PEER. 50:50 doesn't count people integral to the story, like the Prime Minister giving a speech or the only eyewitness to a bomb. Everyone else counts: analysts, academics, experts and case studies. Students would decide which sources in a story they had a choice over. Usually that means power and key witnesses are fixed, but expertise, representatives and most case studies aren't.

**Adds to existing questions**
3. **Level 5, track your sources.** Add 50:50 as the real example. Possible extra wrong answer: changing your habits during your first month of counting. 50:50 asks teams not to, so they get a true starting figure.
4. **Level 4 intro.** "Widening the net never lowers the bar" could cite 50:50's rule: "The best contributor is always used", even at a cost to the figures. This supports the tokenism wrong answers.
5. **Sport questions** (girls quitting football, ticket prices, cup final). Add an ABC Sport tip: when covering men's sport, bring women in as experts, and profile everyday players, not just elite athletes.
6. **Rural hosepipe question (4.7).** Add BBC Radio Devon's experience as a tip. Over about a year it doubled its contributors from ethnically diverse backgrounds, and it notes that in rural areas people "may feel much more visible and often more isolated".
7. **Health stories** (A&E, long Covid). At the start of Covid, ABC found health leaders skewed male, so it sought frontline voices such as nurses, care workers and teachers. This could be a "Widen the net" tip.

**Audit page and resources**
8. **Audit page.** Compare the game with the BBC's targets as well as the Census: 50% women, 20% Black, Asian and minority ethnic, 12% disabled. The game hits 12% disability exactly, and its ethnicity figure is well over 20%. The page could also say that, like 50:50, the audit counts by perception, and add a link to the 50:50 Project to the resources list.

Which would you like? I'd suggest 1 (needed for accuracy), 2, 3 and 8.

## Prompt 22

> How could you split level 5

### Response 22

Level 5 has two kinds of question mixed together. Six are about how you treat a source once you've found them, and six are about your newsroom habits and how you keep count. That splits it into two levels of six.

**New level 5: "Judge the source, not the label"** (how you treat people)
- Interest rates: ask the economist about her expertise, not her identity
- Care homes: an accent isn't a measure of expertise
- Gene therapy: women appear only as experience, while men are the experts
- Building trades: think intersectionally and ask how people want to be described
- Autistic researcher: make reasonable adjustments
- Trans pensions economist: ask about pensions, and don't out her

**New level 6: "Check your habits"** (process and counting)
- Track your sources
- Measure what you control
- Only the men replied: who you contact in the first place
- Death after police restraint: defaulting to official sources on deadline
- Visa rules: two politicians are still one type of voice
- Counting disabled sources: don't guess from photos

The game would then have 8 levels. The two drag-and-drop levels become 7 and 8, with points unchanged. Both new levels could stay at 3 points, or level 6 could go up to 4 so the scoring still climbs.

**Another option** is to split by stage of reporting: before the interview (finding and contacting), during the interview (questions and adjustments) and after it (counting). That gives three levels of four questions each, but makes the game longer overall.

I'd go with the first split. Shall I do it?

## Prompt 25

> I don't want one team to be playing so long that the other team gets bored or it's unfair. Update the game so that a team can get a maximum of 3 questions right in a row. If one team gets 3 questions in a row correct, play switches to the other team. Make sure the game tells the player this rule, and informs then when play is switching because they have got 3 in a row (congratulate them)

### Response 25

In Blue v Red mode, a team can now answer at most 3 questions in a row before play passes to the other team.

**Where players see the rule:**
- **Set-up screen:** the rules text now says that after 3 correct answers, play passes to the other team.
- **Level intro:** "A wrong answer, or 3 right in a row, passes play."
- **During a turn:** a small "1 of 3 in a row" or "2 of 3 in a row" counter sits next to the team's turn badge.
- **After each correct answer:**
  - First or second in a row: "Blue team keeps the turn (2 of 3 in a row)".
  - Third in a row: "Three in a row, well done Blue team! Over to Red team."

The count goes back to zero whenever play changes hands, and it carries over from one level to the next. Solo mode is unchanged.
